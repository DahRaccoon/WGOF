The 2026 Flynnvitational

Indian Tree Golf Course Crane, Missouri Oct 17th 15 Holes + 1/2/5 Replay
Flynnvitational Special Rules
Holes 1, 2 & 5 are played twice — but the replay comes with consequences.
  1. No repeat clubs: when you replay holes 1, 2 and 5, you cannot use any club you used on that hole the first time.
  2. The Traveling Ball: one special golf ball changes hands every hole. Everyone gets it three times over the 15-hole loop.
  3. Working Together for 69: if the Traveling Ball scores 69 or better after 15 holes, everyone gets their putter back.
  4. Birdie earns a club: any player who makes birdie while playing the Traveling Ball gets one club back for 1 hole on the final 3.
  5. Lose to the ball: if at any point your score is losing to the Traveling Ball, you owe 10 push-ups on the tee box before your shot.

Tournament Preview

Club Chaos Comes to Indian Tree

The WGOF heads to Indian Tree in Crane for Keith Flynn's Flynnvitational, where a 15-hole layout becomes an 18-hole test by replaying holes 1, 2 and 5. The catch: on the replay, every club used the first time through that hole is off limits. Add a Traveling Ball that can restore clubs, return everyone's putters, or send somebody to the tee box for 10 push-ups, and the standings could get weird in a hurry. Keith enters fourth in the Cup race with home-event bragging rights on the line — and a format designed to make everyone think twice before pulling a favorite club.

Points Leader

Kyle Has the Lead — and Ryan Has His Attention

Kyle leaves Dream Valley with the season lead at 3,150 points, but not the trophy. His 83 was the low gross round at the Edwards Invitational and his birdie on 18 forced the showdown, only for Ryan to take the playoff. Now the Cup leader heads to Indian Tree with just a 100-point cushion over Devon and a format built to take trusted clubs out of his hands.

100 Points Back

Devon Is Still Right on Kyle's Heels

Devon's brutal 18th at Dream Valley turned a possible title run into a tie for third, but the bigger picture remains strong: 3,050 points and second in the season standings, only 100 behind Kyle. He survived an entire lost sleeve last time and stayed competitive anyway. At Indian Tree, avoiding another late-round disaster could be enough to put him on top of the Cup race.

Looking for a Jump

Sterling Needs the Flynnvitational to Shake Things Up

Sterling's steady stream of bogeys at the Edwards Invitational kept the card from exploding, but fifth place left him fifth in the Cup at 2,300 points. The Flynnvitational may be exactly the kind of chaos he needs: restricted clubs, a Traveling Ball and bonus opportunities that can erase the advantage of pure scoring. Steady Eddy gets a chance to turn steady into dangerous.

Defending Champion

Ryan Arrives With a Trophy and a Target

Ryan finally broke through at his own tournament, surviving another 18th-hole adventure before the Top-Flite bonus ball helped force a tie and he beat Kyle in the playoff. The win vaulted him to third in the standings at 2,900 points, just 250 behind the lead. The question at Indian Tree is simple: was Dream Valley the breakthrough, or a one-week escape from the 18th-hole curse?
